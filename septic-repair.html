Septic repairs start with figuring out why it failed.

Backups, alarms, slow drains, wet ground and drain field trouble can come from different parts of the system. Bulldog Septic handles repair work from minor issues to larger system problems.

Common septic problems

Get the actual problem identified before digging up more than you need to.

A septic system is more than a tank. Pipes, pumps, distribution components and the drain field all have to work together.

Backups & slow drainage

Determine whether the issue points to a full tank, blockage, failed component or a larger problem elsewhere in the system.

Drain field issues

Evaluate saturated areas, surfacing wastewater and other signs that the absorption area may not be working correctly.

Pumps & alarms

Address alarm conditions and pump-system problems where applicable, including troubleshooting before replacement.

Damaged system components

Repair or replace failed piping, lids, risers, distribution components and other accessible septic-system parts as needed.

Repair before replacement

Not every septic problem means a whole new system.

The right fix depends on what failed, why it failed and the condition of the rest of the system.

Signs to call

Do not ignore a septic system that is warning you.

Sewage backing up into sinks, tubs or toilets
Septic alarm sounding
Strong sewage odors indoors or outside
Unusually wet, lush or soft ground near the tank or drain field
Repeated clogs or slow drainage throughout the house

FAQ

Septic repair questions.

Does a backup always mean the drain field has failed?

No. A backup can have several causes, including a full tank, blockage, pump problem or drain field issue. Diagnosis matters before assuming the worst.

Can a drain field be repaired?

Some drain field problems can be addressed with targeted repair, while others may require replacement or redesign. The property and failure mode determine the options.

What should I do if my septic alarm is going off?

Limit unnecessary water use and call for service. An alarm can indicate a high-water condition or a problem with a pump, float or control component.
